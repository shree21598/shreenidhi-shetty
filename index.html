How I run a deployment

The field
playbook

The same six steps sit behind every case file below. Each step comes with the receipt from a real deployment.

Sit with the people who own the problem

I start in the workflow, with the analysts and engineers who live in it, before picking a model or a framework.

Receipt: partnered with the credit lending team on 5,000 card disputes a month.

Agree on the number that means done

Analyst hours, ticket deflection, p95 latency, acceptance rate. If we can't measure it, we haven't scoped it yet.

Receipt: every case below ends on a metric the customer signed up for.

Build on the stack they already run

Agents and RAG on Azure AI Search, Vertex AI or Databricks, wired into tools like ServiceNow where the work happens.

Receipt: production on Azure, GCP and Databricks; AWS for earlier work.

Prove it with evals before anyone trusts it

Retrieval relevance, groundedness and tool-call correctness, scored automatically, plus adversarial tests for injection and tool misuse.

Receipt: 500-case eval harness; unsafe behavior halved across 100 attack scenarios.

Get it through the gate and into production

Design review, tracing, cost and latency work, regression tests on every mitigation. Then a real pilot with real users.

Receipt: Design Review Board approval and a 12-engineer pilot for the incident agent.

Leave a pattern the next team can reuse

Recurring blockers become modules and reference architectures, and what I learn goes back out as training.

Receipt: modules adopted by 8 teams; workshops for 350+ engineers and PMs.

CASE 01 · NAVY FEDERAL

Card dispute case generation

Customer
Credit lending team (internal)
Volume
5,000 disputes / month
Status
In production
Agentic workflowState persistenceAzure OpenAI

Problem

AI-generated dispute cases had to stay correct as new evidence kept arriving on disputes that were already open. Without that, updates turned into duplicate cases and analysts spent their time cleaning them up.

Shipped

Redesigned case generation with the lending team so case state persists across updates, and new evidence is matched to its case by dispute ID before anything new is created.

Outcome

About 1,000 duplicate cases prevented and 165 analyst hours saved each month.

Enterprise virtual assistant

Customer
Enterprise support, end users of the assistant
Volume

Status
In production

Problem

The assistant's RAG platform had to carry real enterprise traffic and actually close requests, at a cost and latency the business would keep paying for.

Shipped

Productionized the platform on Azure AI Search and Azure OpenAI. Then I traced retrieval and generation end to end in LangSmith and KQL, found the bottlenecks and fixed them one at a time.

Outcome

Incident resolution agent

Customer
On-call engineering teams
Knowledge
Runbooks + 2,000 past incidents
Status
DRB approved · 12-engineer pilot
LangGraphServiceNow toolsRAGHuman in the loop

Problem

Engineers needed fix recommendations grounded in the team's runbooks and incident history, delivered inside ServiceNow where they already work.

Shipped

Architected and built a LangGraph agent that calls ServiceNow tools and retrieves over runbooks and 2,000 historical incidents to recommend a fix. Took it through Design Review Board approval, then piloted it with 12 engineers who reviewed every suggestion.

Outcome
